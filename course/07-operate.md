## The window is the test

During a Type II window, every control has to run every time. The work is easy to forget, and the people who set it up move on. Schedule it, give it an owner, and keep it visible.

## The calendar

Put each recurring control on a shared calendar with an owner and a backup. The [year-round calendar](../plan/observation-calendar.md) gives a starting layout by month, with the every-event controls listed below it.

## Running an access review

1. Export the current, dated user list from each in-scope system.
2. Send each list to the system owner with a one-week deadline.
3. The owner marks each account keep or remove, and checks that permissions match the person's role.
4. Remove what's marked, with a ticket for each removal.
5. File the lists, the marks, the tickets, and the owner's sign-off together. The [access review template](../templates/access-review.md) has the form.

The most common finding is accounts of people who left months ago, still active in a tool nobody thought of. Check that your departure checklist covers every system in the inventory.

## Running a tabletop

Once a year, gather the incident response team for an hour and talk through a scenario, such as "A customer emails to say their data appeared on a public forum." Who's called? Who decides to tell customers? Where are the logs? Fix what was slow or unclear. The notes, the attendee list, and the follow-up tickets are the evidence for [INC-03](../controls/controls-map.md#incidents).

## When something changes

- **An owner leaves.** Reassign their controls the same week and update the calendar.
- **A new system arrives.** Add it to the inventory, the access reviews, the departure checklist, and the vendor list.
- **A new vendor touches customer data.** Review it before signing, not after.
- **A control changes**, say to a new device management tool. Record the date, keep evidence from before and after, and tell the auditor.

## Exercise: build the calendar

Create the shared calendar with every recurring control, each with an owner and a backup. Book the next quarterly access review. Pick a tabletop scenario from your risk register and book the session.
