## What policies are for

A policy says what the company requires. Controls show it happens. Auditors read your policies first, then test whether your controls match. The most common problem is a downloaded template promising things the company has never done. About ten documents cover a small company, and one handbook is fine. See the [policy set outline](../templates/policy-outline.md).

## Writing them so they get read

- **One to three pages each.** Long policies get skimmed, then ignored.
- **Say "we" and "you."** "You report a lost laptop to IT within one hour" beats "Personnel shall notify the IT function."
- **State the rule, not the tool.** "Production access requires two-step sign-in" survives a change of provider. Name tools in procedures.
- **Match the controls.** If the policy says "quarterly," so does the control.
- **Put an owner, version, approval date, and next review date** at the top.

## Approval and acknowledgment

1. Leadership approves the set. A dated email or meeting note naming the documents and versions is evidence for [GOV-03](../controls/controls-map.md#governance).
2. Publish where staff work, linked from onboarding.
3. Everyone acknowledges at hire and each year ([GOV-04](../controls/controls-map.md#governance)). A form that records name and date is enough.
4. Review yearly and when something big changes, and record the review even when nothing changes.

## Exercise

Draft the access control and incident response policies first, because they shape the most controls, and match every promise to your controls list from [module 4](04-controls.md). Outline the rest, with an owner and a due date for each.
