## Choosing an auditor

Only a licensed CPA firm can issue a SOC 2 report. Talk to at least three, and ask:

- How many SOC 2 reports did you issue last year for companies our size and type?
- Who does the fieldwork, and how much access will we have to them?
- Do you offer a readiness assessment, and how do you keep it separate from the audit? A firm that designs or runs your controls can't independently audit them. Some firms do a gaps-only review for a client they later audit, and some companies use a different firm. Ask what safeguards apply.
- How do you collect evidence: your portal, our tracker, or a compliance platform?
- What does the fee include, and what costs extra?
- Can we speak with two clients you audited recently?

Ask your customers which firms' reports they trust. A **readiness assessment** lists design gaps without issuing a report. Fix what it finds, then start the window. It doesn't promise the same auditor will accept your evidence later.

## Fieldwork

For a Type II, fieldwork happens near the end of the window and runs from a few days to a few weeks. Expect a request list of populations, policies, and settings evidence (your tracker should answer most of it on day one), walkthroughs where control owners show the work on screen, sample requests, and follow-up questions. You can respond to possible exceptions before the report is final.

Brief every control owner beforehand: answer what's asked, show the real system, and say "I'll find out" instead of guessing.

## Reading the report

- **The auditor's opinion.** Unqualified (clean) is the goal.
- **Management's assertion:** your statement that the description is accurate and the controls met the criteria.
- **The system description** from [module 2](02-scope.md), refined.
- **Controls, tests, and results,** with any exceptions and your management response.
- **Complementary controls** your customers and subservice organizations are expected to do.

Read it as a customer's security team will, looking for exceptions, scope gaps, and complementary controls.

## After the report

- **Share it under NDA,** usually through a trust page or on request.
- **Keep the window rolling.** Start each twelve-month period when the last one ended, so coverage has no gap.
- **Use bridge letters** to cover the months between periods. It is your own statement that nothing material has changed. The auditor doesn't issue it.
- **Grow on purpose.** Add criteria, such as Availability, when customers need them. The controls map already carries ISO 27001 Annex A references. ISO adds a management system: a scope statement, a statement of applicability (every Annex A control and why it applies or doesn't), a risk treatment plan, internal audits, and management reviews.
