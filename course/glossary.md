title: Glossary
description: "The SOC 2 and audit words used in this course, in plain language."
---

**Bridge letter.** Your own letter covering the time since your last report period, saying nothing material has changed.

**Carve-out.** Naming a vendor in your report without testing its controls, and pointing readers to its own report.

**Complementary user entity controls (CUECs).** What a provider expects its customers to do for the provider's controls to work.

**Control.** Something the company does, on a schedule or on every event, that reduces a risk and leaves proof.

**Criteria.** What a SOC 2 report measures controls against. Security (CC1 through CC9) is always in scope. Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, and Privacy are optional. The AICPA calls them the Trust Services Criteria.

**Evidence.** The record that a control happened: a log, ticket, export, or signed review.

**Exception.** A tested sample where the control didn't work as described, reported with management's response.

**Management's assertion.** Your written statement in the report that the system description is accurate and the controls met the criteria.

**Observation window.** The period a Type II report covers, usually three to twelve months.

**Population.** The complete list of times a control should have run in the window. The auditor samples from it.

**Qualified opinion.** An audit opinion that one or more criteria weren't met. An **unqualified** opinion is the clean one.

**Readiness assessment.** A pre-audit review of control design that lists gaps without issuing a report.

**Subservice organization.** A vendor that performs part of the service you report on, such as your cloud provider.

**System description.** The part of the report describing the service, boundaries, components, and controls.

**Type I / Type II.** A Type I covers control design on one date. A Type II covers design and operation over a window.
